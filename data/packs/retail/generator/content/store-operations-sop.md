# 1. Purpose, scope and roles

This SOP describes how a Lumen store runs from the first key turn in the morning to the last alarm set at night. It
applies to all 24 stores (S01 to S24); Lumen.com fulfillment centers follow SOP-FUL-003. Where a store type has its
own rule, the rule is shown in the table in section 2.

| Role | Accountable for |
|---|---|
| Store Manager | Sales, shrink, labor plan, audit score, approvals above the cashier limit |
| Assistant Manager | Opening and closing, cash office, schedule adherence, returns exceptions |
| Department Lead | Planogram, replenishment and cycle counts for one department |
| Cashier | Till accuracy, returns at the point of sale, loyalty sign-ups |
| Stockroom Associate | Receiving, put-away, click-and-collect picking |
| Asset Protection Partner | Shrink analysis, return pattern review, incident follow-up (regional role) |

# 2. Store types, hours and staffing

Lumen operates four store formats. Mall stores follow the landlord's hours when they are longer. Minimum staffing is
the number of colleagues on the floor and at the till while the store is open to customers.

| Format | Stores | Hours (Mon-Sat) | Sunday | Minimum staffing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flagship | {{stores_flagship}} | 9 am to 10 pm | 10 am to 8 pm | 14 |
| Mall | {{stores_mall}} | Mall hours, 10 am to 9 pm minimum | 11 am to 6 pm | 8 |
| Standalone | {{stores_standalone}} | 9 am to 9 pm | 10 am to 7 pm | 9 |
| Outlet | {{stores_outlet}} | 10 am to 9 pm | 10 am to 7 pm | 7 |

Holiday trading hours are published by Store Operations each October. The Store Manager may not shorten published
hours without Regional Operations Director approval.

# 3. Opening procedure

The opening team is two colleagues, one of whom must be an assistant manager or above. Nobody enters the building
alone.

1. Disarm the alarm and sweep the sales floor, stockroom and loading dock before unlocking the main doors.
2. Collect the till floats from the safe (each till starts at $200.00 in the standard denominations) and count them
   in view of the second colleague. Sign the float sheet.
3. Power up registers, card terminals and handheld scanners; confirm the loyalty lookup works.
4. Review the daily brief in the store system: active promotions, price changes, delivery schedule and any Asset
   Protection notices.
5. Walk the floor. Confirm promotional signage matches the daily brief, fixtures are full, and aisles are clear.
6. Check the click-and-collect shelf and clear any orders that expire today.
7. Unlock the doors at the published opening time. Do not open late for any reason other than safety.

# 4. Closing procedure

1. Announce closing 15 minutes before the published time and 5 minutes before. Do not lock customers out.
2. Close registers, run the end-of-day report and print the till summary for each cashier.
3. Count each till with a second colleague. The counted total is compared with the report.
4. Prepare the deposit. All cash above the next day's float of $200.00 per till goes to the safe, and the deposit is
   taken to the bank on the next business day.
5. Walk the floor for left-behind customers, unattended carts and fitting-room items. Return merchandise to its
   home location.
6. Check all exits, lock the stockroom, set the alarm and leave together.

# 5. Cash handling

- **Float and drops.** Each till starts the day at $200.00. When a till holds more than $500.00 in cash, the cashier
  calls for a safe drop. Drops are done by the assistant manager, who signs the drop slip.
- **Variance tolerance.** A till variance of up to $5.00 over or short is within tolerance. A variance above $5.00 is
  recounted and explained on the cash report. A variance above $25.00 is reported to Regional Finance the same day.
- **Two-person rule.** Safe counts, deposit preparation and till pulls always involve two colleagues.
- **Counterfeit detection.** Every bill of $50 or more is checked with the detection pen. A suspected counterfeit
  is not returned to the customer; follow the incident procedure in section 11.
- **Refunds in cash.** Cash refunds follow the return policy limit of $250.00. Larger refunds are issued by check.

# 6. Returns and exchanges at the point of sale

Returns are governed by the Return and Refund Policy (POL-RET-014) and by any Asset Protection memo in force. The
cashier's job is to apply them consistently, record the right codes, and escalate when a rule requires approval.

## 6.1 Processing steps

1. Greet the customer and ask for the receipt, the order number or the Lumen Rewards ID.
2. Look the order up in the point-of-sale system. If the item was bought online, scan the return barcode from the
   confirmation email or look up by order number.
3. Check the item against the category window in the policy. Gold members have 15 extra days.
4. Inspect the item and its packaging and record the condition code (table below).
5. For Electronics, scan the serial number into the return screen. A return of an Electronics item without a serial
   number needs Store Manager approval.
6. The system calculates the restocking fee from the category and condition. The cashier may not change it; only a
   Store Manager can apply the RF-WAIVE code.
7. Refund to the original payment method, or issue store credit where the rules require it. Print the receipt and
   have the customer sign for refunds above $100.00.
8. Place the item in the returns bin with its return tag. Returns are inspected and re-shelved or sent to the
   returns center within 24 hours.

## 6.2 Condition and reason codes

| Code | Meaning | Restocking fee |
|---|---|---|
| CND-U | Unopened or unused | Never |
| CND-O | Opened or used, not defective | Per policy category table |
| CND-D | Defective or damaged | Never |
| RSN-01 | Changed mind or did not fit the space | Per condition |
| RSN-02 | Not as described | Per condition |
| RSN-03 | Stopped working, damaged or missing parts | Never (CND-D) |
| RSN-04 | Gift duplicate or ordered by mistake | Per condition |

## 6.3 Approvals

Cashiers approve standard returns up to $250.00 per transaction. A Store Manager approves returns from $250.01 to
$1,000.00, any return up to 7 days outside the window, and any restocking fee waiver. Returns above $1,000.00 go to the
Regional Operations Director. Approvals are entered with the manager's override code and are never shared.

# 7. Pricing, promotions and markdowns

- **Price changes** take effect at 6:00 am every Tuesday. The daily brief lists the changes; the department lead
  prints and replaces shelf labels before opening.
- **Promotions** start and end on the dates in the promo calendar. Promotional signage goes up the day before the
  start and comes down the morning after the end. The point-of-sale system applies the discount; colleagues never
  override a promotional price by hand.
- **Markdown cadence.** Clearance items are marked down at 25 percent, then 50 percent after four weeks, then 70
  percent after eight weeks. At 70 percent the item is stickered **Final Sale** and cannot be returned.
- **Planogram resets.** Each department is reset once a quarter on a night shift published by Visual Merchandising.
  Resets are completed before the store opens.

# 8. Inventory control and shrink

Shrink is the difference between book inventory and the count. The chain target is 1.2 percent of net sales; each store
manager receives the store's rate monthly.

| Category | Cycle count frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Electronics | Weekly | High value and high shrink; counted by two colleagues, serial numbers spot-checked |
| Furniture | Monthly | Floor models and stockroom stock reconciled separately |
| Home Decor | Monthly | Breakage logged with photographs |
| Kitchen & Dining | Monthly | Small appliances kept in locked cabinets overnight |
| Bedding & Bath | Quarterly | |
| Apparel | Quarterly | Fitting-room counts at open and close |
| Outdoor & Garden | Quarterly | Seasonal stock counted at the start and end of the season |

**Receiving.** Deliveries are booked by dock appointment. The stockroom associate checks the carton count against the
delivery note, signs for exact counts only, and records damage on the note. Merchandise is put away within 48 hours,
and Electronics within 24 hours.

# 9. Click-and-collect and ship-from-store

- **Click-and-collect.** Orders placed on Lumen.com for store pickup are picked within 2 hours during opening
  hours and held for 5 days. Orders not collected are returned to stock and refunded on day 6.
- **Ship-from-store.** Stores with spare stock may be asked to ship an online order. The cut-off for same-day
  dispatch is 3:00 pm. Orders are packed with the supplied carton and label and handed to the carrier at the daily
  pickup.
- **Substitutions** are never made without the customer's consent. A short-picked order is cancelled for that line.

# 10. Safety

- Walk the floor for hazards at open, midday and close; wet floors get a sign immediately.
- Heavy furniture is moved only with the equipment provided and by two colleagues when it weighs more than 50
  pounds.
- Ladders and stockroom steps are inspected monthly and logged.
- Fire exits are kept clear at all times. The assistant manager runs an evacuation drill twice a year.
- Emergency numbers are posted in the cash office. The Store Manager is the incident commander until the
  Regional Operations Director takes over.

# 11. Incidents and reporting

Any injury, theft, counterfeit bill, robbery, near miss or customer complaint that involves a safety issue is reported
in the incident system within 24 hours. Theft and fraud incidents also go to the Asset Protection Partner by phone
the same day. Colleagues never confront a suspected thief; the priority is the safety of customers and colleagues.

# 12. Training and audits

New colleagues complete the onboarding course within their first 14 days and the point-of-sale and returns module before
working a till alone. Each store is audited once a quarter by its regional operations team on a 100-point checklist
covering opening and closing, cash handling, returns documentation, inventory counts, safety and presentation. A
score below 85 triggers a 30-day action plan and a follow-up visit.
