# manufacturing pack: Atlas Precision Components

A fictional machining manufacturer for NVIDIA Knowledge Foundation. Its plant-operations tables are in DuckDB and its
procedures and reports are documents that Nemotron Parse reads, one scanned. Ask about maintenance procedures, safety, quality
and plant performance, or about which machines are likely to fail next. **Every company, plant, machine, part, customer,
supplier and person is invented; the data is synthetic and for a software demonstration only.** This file is generated by
`generator/build.py` together with `files/`, so its figures always match the data.

Foreign keys: `machines.plant_id`, `sensor_readings.machine_id`, `maintenance_events.machine_id`, `work_orders.plant_id`,
`work_orders.machine_id`, `quality_defects.machine_id`, `quality_defects.work_order_id`, and `plant_id` in both plan sheets.
Column descriptions and keys are declared in `pack.yaml`.

- `sensor_readings` are daily aggregates for each machine on each day it ran (weekdays, less plant holidays, the shared
  24 December to 2 January shutdown and repair days): average vibration in mm/s RMS, maximum temperature, spindle hours and alarm count.
- `maintenance_events` holds 275 planned PM-2 services (`event_type = 'planned'`, `task_code = 'PM-2'`) and 118 unplanned
  repairs (`event_type = 'unplanned'`, `task_code = 'CM'`, with a `failure_code`). PM-1 operator checks are on paper, not in the table.
- `work_orders` are production runs on one machine; `qty_started = qty_good + qty_scrap`; the last work order of a machine can be `in_progress`.
  `qty_scrap` includes the pieces of its scrapped defect records.
- `production_plan_weekly_output.actual_units` is the good units produced in the week, from the work orders' daily output.

### How the data was made

One seeded simulation drives every number. Each machine has a baseline vibration and temperature, a weak-unit multiplier and a PM-2 schedule built
from the intervals in the manual (7 machines are left overdue at 30 September 2026 on purpose). The daily chance of an unplanned failure rises with
the days since the last PM-2 and with machine age. Every failure is preceded by a 14 to 28 day degradation ramp in vibration, temperature or alarms
(depending on the failure code), and harmless near-miss ramps add noise, so a model can learn real signal. Defects and scrap follow the machine's
condition. The simulation runs a month past `as_of`; that month is never written, but it is the ground truth for the two prediction questions.
Two stories are scripted: the bore-diameter escape from MTY-LTH-04 (8D report) and a hydraulic leak on DAY-PRS-03 (scanned round sheet).

### `pm2-interval-hydraulic-press` (DOCUMENTS)

> What PM-2 interval does the preventive maintenance manual set for a hydraulic press, and how often is its hydraulic oil sampled?

The PM-2 interval for a hydraulic press is **60 calendar days** (PM-MAN-001, Table 1), and its hydraulic oil is sampled **every 90 days**. PM-1 operator checks on a press are daily.

### `round-sheet-press-findings` (DOCUMENTS)

> On the scanned PM-1 round sheet for press DAY-PRS-03 dated 14 September 2026, what oil temperature did the operator record, and what action was raised for the ram seal?

The operator recorded **63 C** for the oil temperature at working load, above the 62 C limit printed on the sheet, so the item is marked NOT OK. For the ram seal the sheet notes a weep at the ram seal with the drip tray half full (also NOT OK) and the action **maintenance request MR-2287: replace the ram seal kit and check the pump**, with the press on light duty until fixed. Three items are marked NOT OK: oil temperature, ram seal leak and pump noise. The tables agree with the sheet: DAY-PRS-03 reached 63.2 C on 14 September and logged an unplanned HYD-LEAK event (ME-00378) on 17 September 2026.

### `lockout-lock-removal` (DOCUMENTS)

> Under the lockout/tagout procedure, who may remove a lock applied by an authorized employee who is not on site, and what must happen before they do?

Only the **Plant EHS Manager (or a deputy named in writing)** may remove it (SAF-SOP-007, section 7); supervisors and co-workers may not. Before the removal: (1) the Maintenance Supervisor verifies that the employee is not on site and makes at least **two documented attempts to reach them by phone**; (2) the EHS Manager confirms the machine is safe to re-energize and nobody is working on it; (3) the employee is told the lock was removed **before they return to work on that machine**; (4) the removal is entered in the lockout log with the names and the reason.

### `unplanned-downtime-by-plant` (SQL)

> Which plant had the most unplanned downtime hours in Q3 2026 (1 July to 30 September), and how many hours and unplanned events was that?

**Dayton Plant**, with **237.8 hours** of unplanned downtime over 12 events.

| Plant | Unplanned events | Downtime hours |
|---|---|---|
| Dayton Plant | 12 | 237.8 |
| Monterrey Plant | 8 | 185.2 |
| Brno Plant | 5 | 113.5 |

```sql
SELECT p.plant_name, COUNT(*) AS events, ROUND(SUM(e.downtime_hours), 1) AS downtime_hours
FROM maintenance_events e
JOIN machines m ON m.machine_id = e.machine_id
JOIN plants p ON p.plant_id = m.plant_id
WHERE e.event_type = 'unplanned' AND e.started_at >= '2026-07-01' AND e.started_at < '2026-10-01'
GROUP BY p.plant_name ORDER BY downtime_hours DESC;
```

### `top-failure-codes` (SQL)

> Which three failure codes caused the most unplanned downtime hours between 1 October 2025 and 30 September 2026, and how many hours did each cause?

The top three failure codes by unplanned downtime hours, 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026:

| Failure code | Failure | Events | Downtime hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPN-BRG | Spindle bearing wear | 22 | 1088.7 |
| PMP-WEAR | Hydraulic pump wear | 6 | 232.6 |
| SRV-DRV | Servo drive fault | 18 | 224.3 |

The fourth is COOL-PMP with 118.2 hours.

```sql
SELECT failure_code, COUNT(*) AS events, ROUND(SUM(downtime_hours), 1) AS downtime_hours
FROM maintenance_events
WHERE event_type = 'unplanned' AND started_at >= '2025-10-01' AND started_at < '2026-10-01'
GROUP BY failure_code ORDER BY downtime_hours DESC LIMIT 3;
```

### `plan-attainment-q3` (SQL)

> Over the 12 weeks starting 6 July through 21 September 2026, which plant had the lowest production plan attainment (total actual units divided by total target units), and what was it?

**Monterrey Plant** had the lowest attainment, **95.8%**.

| Plant | Actual units | Target units | Attainment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monterrey Plant | 137,845 | 143,900 | 95.8% |
| Dayton Plant | 150,838 | 151,100 | 99.8% |
| Brno Plant | 110,628 | 109,800 | 100.8% |

```sql
SELECT p.plant_name, SUM(w.actual_units) AS actual, SUM(w.target_units) AS target,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(w.actual_units) / SUM(w.target_units), 1) AS attainment_pct
FROM production_plan_weekly_output w JOIN plants p ON p.plant_id = w.plant_id
WHERE w.week_start BETWEEN '2026-07-06' AND '2026-09-21'
GROUP BY p.plant_name ORDER BY attainment_pct;
```

### `overdue-pm2` (HYBRID)

> Using the PM-2 intervals in the preventive maintenance manual, which machines are overdue for their PM-2 service as of 30 September 2026, and by how many days?

**7 machines** are past their PM-2 interval at 30 September 2026 (days since the last PM-2 minus the Table 1 interval for the machine type):

| Machine | Plant | Type | Last PM-2 | Days since | Interval | Days overdue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MTY-GRD-02 | Monterrey Plant | Surface Grinder | 2026-05-28 | 125 | 90 | 35 |
| BRN-LTH-03 | Brno Plant | CNC Lathe | 2026-04-30 | 153 | 120 | 33 |
| DAY-LTH-02 | Dayton Plant | CNC Lathe | 2026-05-19 | 134 | 120 | 14 |
| MTY-MIL-01 | Monterrey Plant | CNC Mill | 2026-06-18 | 104 | 90 | 14 |
| DAY-PRS-04 | Dayton Plant | Hydraulic Press | 2026-07-22 | 70 | 60 | 10 |
| BRN-PRS-01 | Brno Plant | Hydraulic Press | 2026-07-23 | 69 | 60 | 9 |
| DAY-MIL-02 | Dayton Plant | CNC Mill | 2026-06-23 | 99 | 90 | 9 |

Intervals from PM-MAN-001, Table 1: CNC Mill 90, CNC Lathe 120, Hydraulic Press 60, Robot Cell 180 and Surface Grinder 90 days.

```sql
SELECT m.machine_id, m.machine_type, MAX(e.started_at)::DATE AS last_pm2,
       DATE_DIFF('day', MAX(e.started_at)::DATE, DATE '2026-09-30') AS days_since
FROM machines m
JOIN maintenance_events e ON e.machine_id = m.machine_id AND e.task_code = 'PM-2'
GROUP BY m.machine_id, m.machine_type;  -- then compare days_since with the interval for the type
```

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*), SUM(downtime_hours) FROM maintenance_events
WHERE machine_id = 'MTY-LTH-04' AND event_type = 'unplanned' AND started_at >= '2026-07-01';

```
