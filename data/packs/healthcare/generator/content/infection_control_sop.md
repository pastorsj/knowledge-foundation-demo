# 1. Purpose

This procedure describes the practices that prevent the spread of infection among patients, visitors and staff at
Riverside Health Network: hand hygiene, standard and transmission-based precautions, device bundles, environmental
cleaning and outbreak response. It applies to Riverside Memorial Hospital (F01), Lakeview Community Hospital (F02),
Eastgate Regional Hospital (F03) and the twelve affiliated clinics.

# 2. Hand hygiene

Staff clean their hands with alcohol-based hand rub, or with soap and water when hands are visibly soiled, at five
moments: before touching a patient; before a clean or aseptic procedure; after body fluid exposure risk; after
touching a patient; and after touching the patient's surroundings. Soap and water is always used after caring for a
patient with known or suspected *Clostridioides difficile* infection, because alcohol does not kill spores.

Hand hygiene is audited monthly on every unit by trained observers, with at least 30 observations per unit. A unit
below 90% compliance receives a coaching visit within 14 days and is re-audited the following month.

# 3. Standard and transmission-based precautions

Standard precautions apply to every patient. The table lists the added precautions for common conditions. A
precaution sign is posted at the room door before the patient arrives on the unit, and the isolation flag is set in
the patient's electronic record.

| Condition or organism | Precaution | Required PPE | Discontinue when |
|---|---|---|---|
| MRSA or VRE, colonized or infected | Contact | Gown and gloves on room entry | Per Infection Prevention review; at least 3 negative screens for MRSA decolonization |
| *Clostridioides difficile* | Contact plus soap-and-water hand hygiene | Gown and gloves; dedicated equipment | 48 hours after diarrhea has resolved |
| Influenza | Droplet | Surgical mask on room entry; eye protection if splash risk | 7 days after onset or 24 hours after fever resolves, whichever is later |
| Pulmonary tuberculosis, suspected or confirmed | Airborne | N95 respirator (fit tested); negative-pressure room | Three negative sputum smears and clinical improvement, with Infection Prevention approval |
| Measles or varicella | Airborne | N95 respirator; negative-pressure room; only immune staff | 4 days after rash onset (measles); until all lesions crusted (varicella) |
| Norovirus | Contact | Gown and gloves; soap-and-water hand hygiene | 48 hours after symptoms resolve |

## 3.1 Patients with heart failure or COPD

Patients admitted with respiratory symptoms are screened for influenza and other respiratory viruses on arrival. A
patient with COPD exacerbation and suspected influenza is placed on droplet precautions until the result is back,
and no aerosol-generating nebulizer treatment is given in a shared room.

# 4. Device bundles

## 4.1 Central line (CLABSI prevention)

- Insert using maximal sterile barrier precautions and chlorhexidine skin preparation.
- Review the need for the line every day on rounds and remove it as soon as it is not needed.
- Scrub the needleless connector for 15 seconds before each access.
- Change the dressing every 7 days for transparent dressings and immediately if soiled, loose or damp.
- Bathe adult ICU patients daily with chlorhexidine.

## 4.2 Urinary catheter (CAUTI prevention)

- Insert only for an approved indication (acute retention, strict output monitoring in critical illness, surgery,
  or pressure injury healing support) and document it.
- Keep the collection bag below the bladder and never on the floor.
- A nurse-driven protocol removes the catheter within 24 hours of the indication ending.

# 5. Environmental cleaning

| Area | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Patient rooms, high-touch surfaces | Daily, and at every shift change in isolation rooms | Hospital-grade disinfectant, contact time as labeled |
| Rooms of patients with *C. difficile* | Daily, and at discharge | Sporicidal agent (bleach-based) |
| Emergency department bays | After every patient | Disinfectant wipe-down and linen change |
| Clinic exam rooms | Between patients | Disinfectant wipe-down of table, chair and counters |
| Shared equipment (scales, monitors, wheelchairs) | Between patients | Disinfectant wipe |

# 6. Outbreak response

A cluster is two or more patients on one unit with the same organism or syndrome within 7 days. Any staff member who
suspects a cluster calls Infection Prevention at once. Infection Prevention then:

1. confirms the cluster and starts a line list within 4 hours;
2. notifies the hospital's chief nursing officer and the facility leader the same day;
3. places affected patients on the right precautions and cohorts them where possible;
4. reports to the public health department when the condition is reportable, within the time the department sets;
5. holds an outbreak meeting within 24 hours and every working day until no new case has been found for two
   incubation periods.

# 7. Surveillance and reporting

Infection Prevention counts central line-associated bloodstream infections, catheter-associated urinary tract
infections and hospital-onset *C. difficile* each month for each of the three hospitals and reports the standardized
infection ratio to the Infection Control Committee every quarter. Units with a ratio above 1.0 for two quarters in a
row write an improvement plan within 30 days.

# 8. Staff health

Staff with vomiting, diarrhea, a fever or a new rash do not work with patients until cleared by Occupational Health.
Annual influenza vaccination is required for all staff, with documented exemptions reviewed by Occupational Health.
