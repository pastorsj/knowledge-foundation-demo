# 1. Purpose

Prior authorization (PA) is the payer's approval, obtained before a service is delivered, that the service is covered
and medically necessary. A claim for a service that needed approval and did not have it is denied, and the denial
code is "Prior authorization missing". This policy lists the services that need approval, the time a payer has to
decide, and who at Riverside Health Network does what.

# 2. Scope

The policy applies to services ordered for patients covered by Medicare Advantage (Northstar Advantage), Medicaid,
Evergreen Health Plan and Cascade Mutual. Original Medicare does not use prior authorization for most services on this
list, and self-pay patients are quoted a price instead. The Utilization Management team keeps the payer-specific
exceptions in the payer portal matrix; where a payer's contract differs from this policy, the contract controls.

# 3. Services that require prior authorization

The decision turnaround is counted in business days from the time the payer receives a complete request. An
expedited request is for a patient whose health or ability to regain function would be put at serious risk by the
standard wait; the ordering provider must attest to this on the request.

| Service category | Examples | Prior authorization | Standard decision | Expedited decision | Submit at least |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Advanced imaging | MRI, CT, PET scans, cardiac MRI | Required | 5 business days | 24 hours | 5 business days before the scan |
| Elective inpatient admission | Planned surgery, scheduled medical admission | Required | 3 business days | 24 hours | 7 business days before admission |
| Outpatient surgery | Ambulatory surgery and endoscopic procedures | Required | 5 business days | 48 hours | 7 business days before the procedure |
| Cardiac rehabilitation | Phase II supervised exercise program | Required | 7 business days | 72 hours | 10 business days before the first session |
| Home health services | Skilled nursing and therapy visits after discharge | Required | 2 business days | Same day | At discharge planning, before the patient leaves |
| Durable medical equipment over $500 | Home oxygen, hospital bed, powered mobility | Required | 3 business days | 24 hours | 5 business days before delivery |
| Specialty infusion drugs | Biologic and high-cost infused therapies | Required | 3 business days | 24 hours | 5 business days before the first dose |
| Sleep studies | Overnight polysomnography | Required | 5 business days | Not available | 7 business days before the study |
| Physical therapy beyond 12 visits | Continuing therapy after the initial 12 visits | Required | 3 business days | Not available | 3 business days before visit 13 |
| Emergency admission through the ED | Any admission that begins in an emergency department | Not required; notify the payer | Not applicable | Not applicable | Within 24 hours of admission |
| Office visits, laboratory tests, X-ray, ECG, echocardiogram | Primary care and specialist visits, routine tests | Not required | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |

# 4. How to request authorization

1. The ordering provider documents the clinical reason in the order. The order is not released to scheduling until the
   authorization status is "approved" or "not required".
2. The Utilization Management coordinator submits the request through the payer's portal or by fax on the same day
   the order is signed, attaching the clinical notes the payer's criteria require.
3. The coordinator records the reference number and the decision due date in the electronic record and checks the
   status daily until a decision arrives.
4. If the payer asks for more information, the coordinator returns it within 2 business days. The payer's clock
   restarts when the information arrives.
5. If the request is denied, the ordering provider decides within 2 business days whether to appeal or to change
   the order. A peer-to-peer call is requested for every denial of an advanced imaging or elective inpatient request.

# 5. Expedited requests

A request may be marked expedited when the ordering provider attests that the standard wait could seriously
jeopardize the patient's life, health or ability to regain maximum function. Examples are a cardiac MRI for a patient
with new symptoms and a rising troponin, or home oxygen for a patient who cannot be discharged without it. The
coordinator calls the payer's utilization line after submitting and notes the call in the record.

# 6. When authorization is missing

If a service was delivered without a required authorization the coordinator asks the payer for a retrospective
authorization within 14 days of the service. Where the service was an emergency, the emergency notification rule in
section 3 applies and no authorization is needed. Claims that are denied for "Prior authorization missing" are sent
to Denials Management and are written off only after the appeal window in the payer's contract has closed.

# 7. Monitoring

Utilization Management reports monthly to the Revenue Cycle Committee: requests submitted, the share decided within
the turnaround in section 3, expedited requests, and denials by reason. A payer that exceeds its turnaround on more
than 10% of requests in a quarter is raised with the payer's contract manager.
