## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets the standards under which Harborview Community Bank ("the Bank") originates, prices, monitors and
collects consumer loans. It applies to every personal loan, auto loan and residential mortgage made to an individual
through any of the Bank's 18 branches, the online application channel or the loan servicing center. Small business
lending, commercial real estate and credit cards are governed by separate policies.

The policy has three goals: lend only to borrowers who can repay from their income, price each loan for the risk it
carries, and give borrowers who are in temporary hardship a documented way back to a current loan before the Bank
has to charge it off.

## 2. Governance and approval authority

The Board Credit Committee approves this policy once a year and whenever a limit in section 3 changes. The Chief
Credit Officer owns the policy and reports exceptions, delinquency and charge-offs to the Credit Risk Committee every
quarter.

Credit decisions are made within the authority shown in Table 1. Authority is set by the total of the loan being
approved plus all other credit the borrower already has with the Bank. A lender may never approve a loan for a
relative, an employee reporting to the lender or a business the lender owns.

**Table 1. Lending authority by approver**

| Approver | Personal loans (unsecured) | Auto loans | Mortgages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch lender | Up to $15,000 | Up to $40,000 | Not authorized |
| Regional credit officer | Up to $35,000 | Up to $75,000 | Up to $400,000 |
| Credit Committee | $35,001 to $50,000 | Not applicable | $400,001 to $750,000 |

## 3. Underwriting standards

### 3.1 Products and limits

**Table 2. Product limits**

| Product | Amount | Maximum term | Collateral |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal loan | $2,500 to $50,000 | 60 months | Unsecured |
| Auto loan | $8,000 to $75,000 | 72 months | First lien on the vehicle |
| Mortgage | $90,000 to $750,000 | 360 months | First lien on an owner-occupied principal residence |

### 3.2 Credit score bands, debt-to-income and loan-to-value limits

Every applicant is placed in a credit band using the middle of the three bureau scores, or the single score when only
one is available. The band sets the maximum debt-to-income ratio (DTI) and the maximum loan-to-value ratio (LTV) the
Bank will accept. Table 3 shows the limits that apply to applications taken on or after 1 July 2026.

Debt-to-income is the borrower's total monthly debt payments, including the payment on the proposed loan, divided by
gross monthly income. The maximum DTI in Table 3 applies to all three products. Loan-to-value is the loan amount
divided by the lesser of the purchase price and the appraised value (mortgage) or the retail value in the Bank's
valuation guide (auto). A personal loan has no LTV limit. DTI and the credit band are recorded once, at origination,
and are not refreshed during the life of the loan.

### 3.3 Income and identity verification

Income must be verified before approval with the two most recent pay stubs, the last two years of tax returns for
self-employed borrowers, or benefit award letters for retirees. Identity is verified under the Bank's Customer
Identification Program described in the KYC and AML procedure. A loan may not fund until both are complete.

### 3.4 Exceptions

A loan above the maximum DTI or LTV in Table 3 may be approved only by the regional credit officer, and only when at
least two compensating factors are documented: twelve months of liquid reserves, a customer relationship of five
years or more, a co-borrower with a credit score in a higher band, or a down payment of at least 20 percent.
Approved exceptions may not exceed 5 percent of originations by count in any quarter. Every exception is reported to
the Credit Risk Committee with the borrower's band, the ratio approved and the factors relied on.

## 4. Pricing

Rates follow a five-tier risk-based pricing grid that maps one-to-one to the credit bands: Tier A for Exceptional
through Tier E for Poor. The Asset and Liability Committee reviews the grid each month and publishes it to branch
lenders. A lender may not discount a rate by more than 25 basis points without regional credit officer approval, and
no loan may be priced to a customer on the basis of a protected characteristic.

## 5. Delinquency management and charge-off

Collections activity starts the day after a payment is missed and escalates as the loan ages. Table 4 summarizes the
required actions. Days past due are counted from the due date of the oldest unpaid installment.

**Table 4. Collections actions by days past due**

| Days past due | Status | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 14 | Grace period | Automated reminder by text message or email |
| 15 to 29 | Early delinquency | Late fee assessed; collector calls within 5 business days |
| 30 to 59 | Delinquent | Written notice; hardship screening offered; reported to the credit bureaus as 30 days late |
| 60 to 89 | Seriously delinquent | Collections case opened; hardship review; modification considered |
| 90 or more | Default | Placed on non-accrual; referred to Special Assets for a recovery plan |

A personal or auto loan is charged off when it reaches 120 days past due. A mortgage is evaluated for charge-down at 180
days past due, to the appraised value less the cost to sell. Recoveries after charge-off are credited to the
allowance for loan losses. A loan on a hardship plan that is being followed is not charged off while the plan lasts.

The Bank defines default for reporting purposes as 90 or more days past due, and delinquency as 30 or more days past
due. Delinquency rates are reported as the number of active loans with at least one installment unpaid for 30 or more
days divided by the number of active loans at the reporting date. Charged-off loans are excluded.

## 6. Hardship and loan modification

### 6.1 Eligibility

A borrower may request a modification when a documented hardship (job loss, reduction in work hours, serious illness,
the death of a co-borrower or a declared disaster) makes the contractual payment unaffordable. To be eligible the
borrower must have made at least three payments on the loan, must not be more than 119 days past due on a personal or
auto loan, and must not be in bankruptcy.

### 6.2 How to ask

The request must be made in writing on Form LS-114, Request for Loan Modification, signed and dated by every
borrower, with documentation of the hardship attached. Branch staff scan the signed form and send it to Loan
Servicing, which logs the received date. A verbal request starts the hardship screening in Table 4 but is not a
modification request until the signed form arrives.

### 6.3 What the Bank can offer

The modification options are limited to the following. The Bank does not reduce the interest rate or forgive
principal.

- **Temporary payment reduction.** The monthly payment may be reduced by up to 50 percent for no more than 6 months.
  Payments that are not collected are added back to the loan balance and the payment is re-amortized when the plan ends.
- **Term extension.** The maturity date may be extended by up to 12 months, once in the life of the loan.
- **Late fee waiver.** Late fees assessed in the 60 days before the request are waived on approval.

### 6.4 Decision and follow-up

Loan Servicing decides within 10 business days of receiving a complete request and tells the borrower in writing. If the
request is denied the letter states the reason and the borrower's other options. An approved plan is documented in
the servicing system, and a plan that is missed twice is closed and the loan returns to normal collections.

## 7. Monitoring and reporting

Each quarter the Credit Risk team reports to the Credit Risk Committee on delinquency by product, the allowance for
loan losses, charge-offs and recoveries, exceptions granted, and the share of recent originations that exceed the
limits currently in force. Loans originated before a change in this policy are not re-underwritten, but the quarterly
report identifies originations from the previous 12 months that would no longer meet the new maximum DTI so that
the Committee can decide whether portfolio action is needed.
